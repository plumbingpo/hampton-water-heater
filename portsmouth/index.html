Keeping Your Portsmouth Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or moisture around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. A quick call saves time, money, and cold showers.
