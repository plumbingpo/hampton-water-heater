How Newport News Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms such as rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
