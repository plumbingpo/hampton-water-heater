Kitchen Hot Water

Hot Water for Kitchens, Restaurants, and Food Facilities

Restaurants and food-service businesses have hot water demands that ordinary equipment can't meet. Dish machines, three-compartment sinks, prep sinks, mop sinks, and handwashing stations all draw at once during a busy dinner service, and a tank with too little recovery runs cold just as the dish room fills up. Hampton Water Heater handles food-service hot water, including high-recovery gas tanks, tankless racks, booster heaters for high-temperature dish machines, proper sizing based on peak-hour demand, and scheduled service that works around your hours.

Hot water temperature is a core issue for any food-service operator. Health inspectors check water temperature at handwashing sinks and sanitizing stations, and a dish machine that can't reach its rinse temperature can stop service. Our work keeps your system delivering the temperatures and flow your operation depends on, with written service records that show a well-maintained kitchen to inspectors and landlords. You get detailed reports after each visit, along with the documentation many commercial leases require.

Equipment replacements and maintenance schedules are planned with owners, managers, and kitchen staff, so everything fits your operating schedule. When a job can't happen during service, we install overnight or before opening, so hot water is ready when the first shift arrives.

Safe Installation

Code-Compliant Installation and Documentation

A poorly installed or neglected water heater can lead to carbon monoxide hazards, scalding, bacterial growth in tanks stored too cool, and costly water damage when a tank fails. Those are real risks for any commercial property owner responsible for employees, customers, and tenants. Our commercial installations and inspections cover venting, combustion air, gas line sizing, relief valve discharge, expansion tanks, mixing valves, and code items like drain pans and seismic straps where required, followed by a written report that ranks each risk by priority and recommends specific fixes.

Good records matter almost as much as good repairs. Every account gets a documentation binder with detailed service logs, temperature readings, and relief valve test results, so you can demonstrate due diligence to landlords, insurers, and inspectors. Equipment that works hardest, in kitchens, laundries, and large mechanical rooms, is put on a more frequent visit schedule.

After-Hours Service

Fast Commercial Water Heater Service

A commercial water heater failure starts costing money the minute it happens. Dish rooms stall, salon chairs empty, guests call the front desk, and tenants start complaining. Our commercial line gets a technician rolling fast for emergency repairs anywhere in Hampton and the surrounding area, and planned work can be booked overnight or before opening to limit downtime.

Commercial emergencies come in many forms. The cause could be a failed gas valve, a burned-out element, a flame sensor that stopped proving flame, a tripped high-limit switch, a leaking tank, a stalled recirculation pump, or a tankless bank throwing error codes. Whatever the cause, we find it fast, restore hot water, and document the entire call for your facility files. Then we put maintenance in place to keep it from happening again.

Ongoing Protection

Commercial Water Heater Maintenance Programs

The most cost-effective way to manage commercial water heaters is preventing failures before they happen. Hampton Water Heater offers customized maintenance programs for commercial properties of all types and sizes. Our programs include scheduled inspections, tank flushing, anode rod checks, tankless descaling, relief valve testing, burner and venting inspection, recirculation pump and mixing valve checks, and temperature verification at the fixtures. Each program is tailored to the property's equipment, water hardness, usage patterns, and budget.

Compared with an emergency water heater replacement, scheduled maintenance costs a fraction as much, and it sharply cuts the risk of downtime and failed inspections.
