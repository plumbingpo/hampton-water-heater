Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Virginia Beach

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so you can reach us fast the moment something seems off. A quick call saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Virginia Beach

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them in Virginia Beach. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless heaters show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
