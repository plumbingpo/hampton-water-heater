How Chesapeake Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually encodes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Chesapeake

Whatever type of water heater your Chesapeake home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tank heaters tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric units burn out elements and thermostats, especially when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale buildup, ignition faults, and flow sensor trouble, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters need clean air filters and a clear condensate drain, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and stock the common parts to finish most repairs in one trip.
